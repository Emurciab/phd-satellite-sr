Remote sensing-based monitoring of a large area such as the Stormwater Treatment Areas (STAs) in South Florida (~250 km²) requires a system capable of covering broad spatial extents while maintaining both high spatial resolution and fine temporal resolution. Satellite-based systems are attractive for this purpose because they provide frequent, large-scale observations, and in some cases, high spatial resolution through commercial providers.

The main problem we are trying to solve is both technical and economic: how to obtain frequent, high-resolution imagery without making monitoring financially prohibitive. This challenge leads to two main scenarios depending on cloud conditions.

In periods of low cloud interference, Sentinel-2’s revisit frequency (~5 days) may be sufficient to capture relevant phenological changes in aquatic vegetation. In this scenario, the main limitation is spatial resolution rather than temporal resolution. A super-resolution (SR) algorithm trained on paired Sentinel-2 and PlanetScope imagery, using Sentinel-2 as the low-resolution input and PlanetScope as the high-resolution target, could enhance the spatial resolution of future Sentinel-2 imagery. This would provide a cost-effective way to improve mapping detail while relying primarily on free Sentinel-2 data.

In periods of high cloud interference, however, Sentinel-2’s temporal resolution becomes a major limitation. It is common to experience gaps of several months without a single usable image. In these conditions, a finer revisit frequency becomes essential because it increases the probability of acquiring cloud-free observations. Commercial constellations such as PlanetScope and WorldView, with near-daily revisit times, can address this temporal limitation by improving the likelihood of obtaining usable imagery. The drawback is that the cost of these data creates a significant economic barrier.

In this second scenario, an SR model trained only on Sentinel-2 and PlanetScope pairs is not sufficient, because future Sentinel-2 imagery may simply not exist for the periods of interest due to persistent cloud cover. Instead, the role of SR changes. Rather than generating high-resolution imagery from free Sentinel-2 data, the goal becomes to maximize the value of purchased commercial imagery. For example, if PlanetScope imagery must already be acquired to ensure temporal coverage, an SR model could be used to further enhance its spatial resolution toward very high resolution products, such as WorldView-like scales. In this case, the Sentinel-2 to PlanetScope SR model could serve as a foundational model, which would then be adapted through transfer learning by fine-tuning it with PlanetScope as the low-resolution input and very high-resolution imagery, such as WorldView, as the target. Because fine-tuning would require fewer training samples than building a model from scratch, this approach could reduce the cost barrier associated with training on expensive very high-resolution imagery.
